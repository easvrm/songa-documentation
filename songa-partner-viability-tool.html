Step 1 — What is the asset used for?

The operating model determines how the platform earns revenue and what assets it needs to provide.

What does the trike carry in the morning?

Select the commodity or commodities. A trike can carry a mix — the tool adds up all commodities into one daily revenue total.

Dairy collection is the core model. Trikes collect raw milk from farmers in the morning and deliver to a dairy cooperative or processor. Revenue is the spread between what the platform pays farmers and the price the offtaker pays.

Step 2 — Afternoon use of the asset

After the morning base load, what does the trike do in the afternoon?

Battery-as-a-Service (BaaS): After the morning run, the trike's battery capacity is rented out to other operators (motorcycle taxis, delivery riders) through battery swaps. The platform earns a swap tariff. The driver operates independently and pays a daily energy fee — this model generates recurring energy revenue without operating a second logistics route.

Step 2 — Hub revenue model

In a pure logistics hub, the driver owns the transport economics — TaaS revenue goes to the driver. The hub's survival depends on BaaS revenue from the charging station. Choose whether the hub also participates in TaaS earnings or runs on BaaS alone.

⚡ BaaS only (pure charging hub): The hub earns exclusively from battery swaps. The driver keeps 100% of transport income. The hub must be viable on charging revenue alone — the more conservative model.

⚡📦 BaaS + TaaS (hub co-earns transport): The hub takes a share of transport tariff revenue in addition to BaaS earnings. The driver receives a commission; the hub keeps the spread. Requires operational oversight but improves hub economics.
Hub revenue model

In BaaS-only mode, transport demand drives electricity consumption and trike wear — it just doesn't generate hub revenue directly.
